The story, in brief

The Enneagram has ancient roots — some trace it back to the desert fathers, others to Sufi traditions — but its modern form took shape in the 20th century. Gurdjieff used the symbol to describe the laws of the universe; Ichazo placed the 9 passions on it; Naranjo turned it into a psychological map; Riso and Hudson made it accessible worldwide.

Professions and backgrounds very different from one another:

George Gurdjieff (1866–1949) — Armenian philosopher and spiritual mystic. Master of sacred dances and teacher of a system of "wise inner work"; he introduced the Enneagram symbol as a diagram of cosmic laws (the 1910s–1920s).

Oscar Ichazo (1931–2020) — Bolivian philosopher, founder of the Arica Institute. He developed the first Enneagram typologies (the nine points of the ego), integrating them into a broader system of consciousness (the 1950s–60s).

Claudio Naranjo (1932–2019) — Chilean psychiatrist and psychotherapist, pupil of Fritz Perls (founder of Gestalt) and researcher at the Esalen Institute. He connected Ichazo’s Enneagram with modern psychology (the 1970s).

Helen Palmer — Psicologa e insegnante, autrice di The Enneagram (1988). Specializzata in comunicazione interpersonale e intuizione.

Don Richard Riso (1946–2012) — Writer, former Jesuit and Benedictine monk. Author of Personality Types (1987); he coined the modern names of the nine types.

Russ Hudson — Psicologo e ricercatore spirituale, coautore e successore di Riso.

Richard Rohr — Francescano, sacerdote, teologo e scrittore spirituale; fece conoscere l’Enneagramma al grande pubblico cattolico con The Enneagram (1995).

Katherine Chernick Fauvre e David W. Fauvre — Researchers who developed the study of subtypes ("tritypes").

Ginger Lapid-Bogda — Psychologist and organisational consultant, specialised in applying the Enneagram in business and coaching.

Tara Bennett-Goleman — Psychotherapist, who connected the Enneagram to mindfulness.

Riso and Hudson: the modern codification

Don Richard Riso e Russ Hudson brought the Enneagram to a wider audience, codifying it into a structured, scientifically oriented system. Specifically, during the 1980s and 1990s:

  • Riso published Personality Types (1987), assigning the nine types the names still most used today (Reformer, Helper, Achiever…) and introducing the Livelli di Sviluppo: a path of 9 levels of health for each type (healthy, average, unhealthy), which makes it possible to describe how a person changes over time, not just which type they are.
  • Hudson joined Riso in the 1990s, and together they founded the Riso-Hudson Enneagram Institute (1997).
  • They developed the RHETI (Riso-Hudson Enneagram Type Indicator), un test di valutazione dei tipi ampiamente diffuso.

Thanks to the Levels of Development, Riso and Hudson went beyond the simple "sorting into 9 boxes": the Enneagram became a dynamic tool, able to describe growth and change over time.
